- Add: « Planet walk » page (`planet-walk.html`, module `planet-walk`), linked from the home page: an astronaut in a NASA-style surface spacesuit (`ui/astronaut/`: pressurized torso, bubble helmet with a reflective gold visor and lamps, life-support backpack, chest control unit, arms and legs bending at elbows and knees with joint bearings and red bands, gloves and boots; walking gait with knee bend and body bob, jump pose; casts a shadow from a sun light that follows the walker) seen over the right shoulder by a following camera that comes closer when a wall or ceiling is in the way (`core/follow-camera.ts`), or a first-person view (button or V key, `ui/view-toggle.ts`), walking on the Moon, Mercury, Mars, Venus or the Earth, each with its real surface gravity (`core/walk-worlds.ts`). Physical key positions (`KeyboardEvent.code`) make Z Q S D on AZERTY and W A S D on QWERTY work the same way: Z and S walk forward and back (Shift held: running at 6 m/s instead of 3), Q and D turn (arrows too), Space jumps; dragging the view with the mouse button held (or a finger) turns and tilts it, while moving the mouse without pressing does nothing. Walking speed (3 m/s), turning speed (2 rad/s) and jump take-off speed (3 m/s) are the same everywhere, so only gravity changes the jump (`core/walker.ts`); a panel gives the gravity, the weight compared to the Earth, the jump height and the time in the air (`core/jump-facts.ts`). Each world has its own ground, rocks, sky, haze and sunlight (`ui/world-appearance.ts`); stars show in the black sky of the Moon and Mercury. A space base stands 32 m ahead of the start on every world (`ui/space-base.ts`), built from the home page's mission equipment models grown to human size (two habitat modules, lab, greenhouse, battery, water and oxygen tanks, electrolyzer, relay antenna, a row of seven solar panels) with a landing pad; rocks keep clear of it. Buildings and rocks are solid (`core/obstacles.ts`): each is a box seen from above with a top height, blocking the walker (0.3 m body radius) when more than 0.35 m above the feet, sliding along walls, climbed like a step when lower, landed on after a jump; walking off an edge makes the walker fall. Obstacles have a bottom height too, so that the walker passes under a lintel and a roof stops a jump (1.8 m body height).
